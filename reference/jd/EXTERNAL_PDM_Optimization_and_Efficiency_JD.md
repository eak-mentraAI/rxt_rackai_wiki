**Product Manager, AI Optimization and Efficiency**
Product Management · Remote / Hybrid

**The opportunity**

RackAI is Rackspace's managed AI inference platform, built for the private and sovereign environments where regulated enterprises can actually run AI. Owning the fleet is only an advantage if we serve it well — the difference between hosting a model and operating it profitably is measured in tokens per GPU-second, time to first token, and cost per outcome.

This is where enterprise AI economics are actually won. A model served on the wrong hardware, at the wrong precision, with the wrong batching, quietly burns margin no one sees. Getting serving efficiency right — across NVIDIA and a maturing AMD stack — is what lets us compete on price at volume and turn committed capacity into a defensible cost floor. Few organizations treat that as a product discipline; this role does.

As Product Manager, AI Optimization and Efficiency you own the product direction for how well we serve. You decide which efficiency gains matter, how we prove them, and how the cost story reaches customers and internal decisions — partnering with inference-performance engineering who own the how. The objective is clear; the route to it is yours to set.

**What you'll do**

- Own the roadmap for serving efficiency across the RackAI model portfolio — every model in the garden, fine-tuned and customer models included — getting each to competitive tokens-per-GPU-second and time-to-first-token on the hardware we have, so it can be served profitably however it reaches demand: direct tenants, the app, or a distribution channel like OpenRouter.
- Own the benchmark-and-measurement product: the harness that establishes, per model and per hardware config, what we can produce and what one million tokens cost — the ground truth every pricing and placement decision reads from, and what lets a new model be launched on evidence rather than guesswork.
- Prioritize across the optimization levers — quantization, KV and prefix caching, continuous batching, tensor and expert parallelism, speculative decoding — by their measured effect on cost, throughput and latency for the workloads actually arriving, not by novelty.
- Own the cost-floor story: turn the fleet's measured cost-per-GPU-hour into a defensible cost-per-million-tokens that underwrites pricing across every route to market and the contracted-capacity economics beneath them — today that number is unestablished, and making it real is the first job.
- Own the efficiency bet across our accelerator mix — including a maturing AMD/ROCm stack — as a product outcome: define where serving economics must land for new hardware to broaden what models we can offer, and how we'll know it got there.
- Own the performance-regression gate — the bar a runtime, quantization or config change must clear on latency, throughput and quality before it reaches production — and decide the throughput-versus-latency-versus-cost trade-offs behind it.

**What you'll bring**

- Eight or more years in product management, including time owning an infrastructure, platform or performance-sensitive product.
- Working fluency in inference or ML serving economics — what drives cost and latency, and why the same model performs differently on different hardware.
- A track record of making decisions from measurement, and of building the measurement when it didn't exist.
- The ability to partner with performance and systems engineers as peers and translate deep technical work into cost and customer outcomes.
- Comfort with unit economics as a first-class product constraint — margin, cost per outcome, revenue per GPU-hour.
- Experience delivering inside regulated, audited or sovereign environments, or the judgment to operate credibly there.

**Nice to have**

- Familiarity with GPU serving runtimes (vLLM, SGLang, TensorRT-LLM, NIM) or accelerator selection.
- Exposure to AMD ROCm or heterogeneous-hardware serving.
- A FinOps or capacity-economics background.
